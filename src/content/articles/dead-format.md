The minidisc occupies an unusual position: too recent to be nostalgic,
too obsolete to be useful, and just cheap enough that nobody bothered to
check what was on them before selling.

## What people leave behind

Roughly a third of what arrives is blank. Another third is somebody's copy
of an album you can hear anywhere. The remaining third is the interesting
part — band practices, radio recordings, a wedding speech, forty minutes
of someone's car journey recorded apparently by accident.

None of it was meant for us. All of it is now, functionally, an archive.

## The ethics bit, briefly

We do not put any of it on the radio without knowing where it came from.
This is a lower bar than it sounds and a surprising number of people
clear it enthusiastically, having assumed the tapes were long gone.
